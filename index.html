How BassCast works

BassCast SA turns the weather forecast into a fishing score for South African bass dams, so you can see which days and hours are worth planning around. Tap a topic to read more.

What the app does
  • Forecast: a score out of 100 for today, your best fishing window, an hour-by-hour chart and the next 7 days. “Go deeper” explains what is behind the score and how to fish it.
  • Map: pick one of the dams or drop your own pin, then save it as a spot with notes.
  • Catch log: record your sessions. Each one is saved with its forecast score, so over time you can see how the score matches what you actually catch.
  • Settings: your home spot, units, how you fish, appearance and backups.
How the score is worked out

Every hour gets a score from five factors. Two of them set how hard bass feed:

  • Water temperature. The biggest factor. Bass slow right down below about 13°C and feed hardest around 25–27°C. Dams have no free water-temperature feed, so the app estimates it from the past week of air temperature, because water warms and cools days behind the air. Warming spells raise the score and sudden cooling lowers it.
  • Fronts. Bass often feed as pressure falls ahead of a cold front, then slow down for a day or two after it, when pressure climbs and the air cools. The app spots fronts from those changes.

The other three decide which hours are best:

  • Light. In warm water, first and last light are best, and cloud helps through the middle of the day. In cold water the warm afternoon is best instead.
  • Wind. A light to moderate breeze (about 7–20 km/h) is ideal. Gales cap the score for safety.
  • Rain and storms. Light rain is fine, heavy rain is not, and thunderstorm hours are capped low because you should be off the water.

Small adjustments are added for the season and spawn stage (worked out from the water temperature and month), the moon and solunar times, and heat stress on hot, still, bright days.

Day score and best window
  • Day score: the average of the day’s best 4 daylight hours, so a great morning makes a great day.
  • Best window: the top-scoring daylight hour, stretched to as much as 3 hours when the hours beside it score almost as well.
  • Bands: Bad under 40, Slow 40–54, Medium 55–67, Good 68–79 and Excellent 80 and up. The Score guide on the Forecast tab explains each one.
Learning from your catch log

Once you have 10 sessions logged with a score, the app starts to tune itself to your fishing. Factors that rise and fall with your catch rate count a little more, and those that do not count a little less.

  • Changes start small and grow only as you add sessions, and they are capped, so a few lucky days cannot take over the score.
  • Log your slow days as well as your good ones. Both are needed to learn what matters.
  • Sessions get a score for dates from 7 days ago to 7 days ahead, so you can log a trip after you get home.
  • “What drives the score” shows how far each factor has been tuned. You can switch learning off above.
What it cannot see
  • The water temperature is an estimate and can be off by a degree or two, more so on very deep or very shallow dams.
  • Water clarity, water level, baitfish and fishing pressure all matter but have no free data, so they are not in the score. Keep them in your spot notes.
  • Forecasts can change. Refresh before you go, and always check local weather and water safety.

Treat the score as a guide to stack the odds, not a promise of fish.
